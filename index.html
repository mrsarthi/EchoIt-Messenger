What makes EchoIt different

A messenger for people who would rather their conversations weren't sitting on someone else's server.

No phone number. No account.

Your identity is created on your device, with a twelve-word recovery phrase that only you hold. Nobody needs your number to reach you, and there is no sign-up.

Windows and Android, together

The same EchoIt on your computer and your phone. A PC and a phone chat like any other pair — messages, photos, files, reactions, replies and read receipts — tested both ways on real devices.

Only your friend can read it

Every message is sealed on your device and opened only on theirs. The helper that introduces your devices can't read a word, and neither can we.

Your history stays with you

Chats are kept on your device, with the words encrypted. No server keeps a copy. Who you talked to and when is not encrypted on the device, so keep a lock screen on.

Files of any size

Send a full-length film if you like. It goes from your device to theirs with no size limit, and on Windows nothing is copied first. Keep EchoIt open until they have it.

You decide who reaches you

People add you with a ticket you share. They can't message you until you accept, and blocking or removing someone happens on your device only.

How it works: built on Dicsussion

Underneath EchoIt is Dicsussion, a messaging engine made by the same developer and published openly, so anyone can check how the important parts work. You never see it — it's what happens when you tap Send.

1

Your identity is made on your device

Dicsussion turns your twelve-word recovery phrase into the keys that prove who you are. They are kept on your device and never sent anywhere.

2

Every message is sealed for one person

It is locked on your device so that only your friend's device can open it, and checked when it arrives, so a changed message would not be accepted.

3

Devices are introduced, then talk directly

A connection helper introduces your device to your friend's. Usually the messages then go straight between the two. When they can't, the helper passes them along — still sealed, so it can't read them.

4

Your chats live with you

Each device keeps its own history. A message waits on your device until your friend's is reachable, then arrives — there is no server in between holding it.

Open for anyone to read. Dicsussion's code is public under the Apache 2.0 licence, on GitHub, and it lists its own known limits there. It has not had an independent security audit yet; when it has, we will say so here. The EchoIt app itself is not open source.

Questions, answered plainly

Is EchoIt free?

Yes. There are no ads and nothing to pay. It is a beta, so expect the odd bug — and tell us when you find one.

Does my friend need EchoIt open to get my message?

Messages arrive while EchoIt is in use on your phone; otherwise the next time both phones are. Until then your message waits on your device, marked as not yet delivered. There is no server holding it in between.

Can you read my messages?

No. Every message is sealed on your device and opened only on your friend's. We have no copy, and neither does the connection helper.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Your twelve-word recovery phrase brings back who you are on a new device. Your message history does not come back: there is no copy anywhere else. Keep the words safe.

Who runs the connection helper?

Number 0, the company behind the networking software EchoIt uses — until mid-November 2026 it is our own. It sees your device's internet address and when it is online, never what you write. The Privacy Policy has the details, and you can use your own helper in Settings.

Windows warns me before installing. Is that normal?

Yes, for now. The Windows installer is not yet signed with a paid certificate, so Windows may say it "protected your PC". Choose More info and then Run anyway. Only download EchoIt from this website or its release page.

Why do I have to allow "unknown sources" on Android?

EchoIt is not on the Play Store yet, so Android asks before installing an app from a website. The app is signed, so later updates install over the top and keep your messages.

Can I make group chats?

Not yet — EchoIt is one-to-one for now.

Is EchoIt open source?

Its engine, Dicsussion, is: anyone can read it on GitHub. The EchoIt app itself is not.
